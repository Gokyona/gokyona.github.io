Life Origins Traversal

Active

Under which conditions does life emerge, and under which does it never emerge?

Instead of running one long simulation and waiting for chance, a computer tests sets of conditions in an ordered, never-repeating sequence and maps the boundary between ordinary chemistry and self-replicating, self-sustaining chemistry: a "phase diagram of life".

  • ✓Minimal stochastic protocell model and an ordered testing engine (archive, Sobol coverage, sequential testing, boundary refinement).
  • ✗Stage 1, 6 conditions: the first engine did not beat random search. Results
  • ✓Stage 1b, 10 conditions: a revised engine pulled clearly ahead of random and Sobol sampling, with one caveat about the shared map-maker. Results
  • ✓Stage 1c, fair re-check: the engine reaches the same accuracy with about 8× fewer simulations than random or Sobol sampling. The gap is smaller than 1b suggested, and it is reported as such. Results
  • ✓Stage 1d: engine made 4× faster on the GPU; a softer point-selection rule becomes the default. Results
  • ✓Stage 1e: on Kauffman's autocatalytic sets (RAF model), a published origin-of-life model with a known threshold, the engine maps the life boundary with about 2× fewer simulations than Sobol sampling. The saving depends on the problem: about 2× here, 8× on our own model. Results · Code
  • ✓Stage 1 paper: the engine, named COMPASS, with all benchmarks and a check against the known threshold for autocatalytic sets: its map recovers the threshold to within 7% on average. Read
  • ✓Stage 2a: a population of dividing protocells (stochastic corrector model) reproduces the known behaviour, and COMPASS maps it with about 8× fewer simulations. Results · Stage 2 plan
  • ✓Stage 2b: protocells whose division is driven by their own chemistry (the cell makes its own membrane and divides when it has doubled), with a new life level L3; COMPASS maps it with about 4× fewer simulations. Results
  • ✓Stage 2d: tried screening with cheap, short simulations. An honest partial negative: it helps only at small budgets, so COMPASS on full simulations stays the default. Results
  • →Stage 2e, next: maps of the conditions for life levels L1–L3, and which conditions each level needs.
Background reading: autocatalytic sets (RAF theory)

The papers behind the stage 1e model, from the original idea to the exact detection algorithm and its known limits.

  1. S. A. Kauffman (1986). Autocatalytic sets of proteins. Journal of Theoretical Biology 119, 1–24. Link
  2. W. Hordijk & M. Steel (2004). Detecting autocatalytic, self-sustaining sets in chemical reaction systems. Journal of Theoretical Biology 227, 451–461. The RAF definition and algorithm we use. Link · PDF
  3. E. Mossel & M. Steel (2005). Random biochemical networks: the probability of self-sustaining autocatalysis. Journal of Theoretical Biology 233, 327–336. Link · arXiv
  4. W. Hordijk, S. A. Kauffman & M. Steel (2011). Required levels of catalysis for emergence of autocatalytic sets in models of chemical reaction systems. International Journal of Molecular Sciences 12, 3085–3101. The "1–2 reactions per molecule" threshold. Open access
  5. V. Vasas, E. Szathmáry & M. Santos (2010). Lack of evolvability in self-sustaining autocatalytic networks constraints metabolism-first scenarios for the origin of life. PNAS 107, 1470–1475. Open access
  6. V. Vasas, C. Fernando, M. Santos, S. Kauffman & E. Szathmáry (2012). Evolution before genes. Biology Direct 7, 1. Open access
  7. W. Hordijk & M. Steel (2016). Autocatalytic sets in polymer networks with variable catalysis distributions. arXiv
  8. W. Hordijk & M. Steel (2017). Chasing the tail: the emergence of autocatalytic networks. BioSystems 152, 1–10. A review. Link
