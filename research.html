Life Origins Traversal: Stage 4 supplementary study

Phase completed

Do the Stage 4 heredity results hold up against measured chemistry and changed model rules?

After Stage 4, I checked how far its heredity results depend on the model's assumptions. Measured copying rates were collected and compared with the model's constants, a copying model at measured rates for a named base pair was built and validated, and the confirmed heredity environments were re-run with built-in rules changed one at a time. Paper 2 was revised with the findings. The study is concluded at this point; the vesicle and protocell levels were not re-tested.

  • ✓Sources and alphabet check: measured copying rates and early-Earth ranges, each source checked. The model's two letters pair as weakly as A·U yet copy as accurately and fast as G·C, which no real base pair does. Alphabet check
  • ✓Copying at measured rates for a named base pair (A with 2-thiouridine; G·C and A·U as controls), with explicit activation chemistry and short hot pulses: built and validated, all 9 tests pass. With measured duplex stability, very short primers rarely extend. Validation
  • ✓Robustness of heredity at the 7 confirmed environments: robust to most simplifications, but it depends in part on the order in which the copy step fills scarce chain slots, a numerical rule. With fair allocation it survives fully at only 1–2 of 7. Results
  • ✓Paper 2 revised (30–31 Oct 2026) with these limits on the heredity results. Paper 2

Life Origins Traversal

Completed

Under which conditions does life emerge, and under which does it never emerge?

Instead of running one long simulation and waiting for chance, a computer tests sets of conditions in an ordered, never-repeating sequence and maps the boundary between ordinary chemistry and self-replicating, self-sustaining chemistry: a "phase diagram of life".

The work has two parts. Part I (stages 0–3) builds and validates the search engine, COMPASS, on a series of test models; the conditions in those models are test conditions, partly properties of the chemistry itself. Part II (stage 4 on) is the real search: which environments give rise to life, using only environmental variables as inputs.

Background reading: autocatalytic sets (RAF theory)

The papers behind the stage 1e model, from the original idea to the exact detection algorithm and its known limits.

  1. S. A. Kauffman (1986). Autocatalytic sets of proteins. Journal of Theoretical Biology 119, 1–24. Link
  2. W. Hordijk & M. Steel (2004). Detecting autocatalytic, self-sustaining sets in chemical reaction systems. Journal of Theoretical Biology 227, 451–461. The RAF definition and algorithm we use. Link · PDF
  3. E. Mossel & M. Steel (2005). Random biochemical networks: the probability of self-sustaining autocatalysis. Journal of Theoretical Biology 233, 327–336. Link · arXiv
  4. W. Hordijk, S. A. Kauffman & M. Steel (2011). Required levels of catalysis for emergence of autocatalytic sets in models of chemical reaction systems. International Journal of Molecular Sciences 12, 3085–3101. The "1–2 reactions per molecule" threshold. Open access
  5. V. Vasas, E. Szathmáry & M. Santos (2010). Lack of evolvability in self-sustaining autocatalytic networks constraints metabolism-first scenarios for the origin of life. PNAS 107, 1470–1475. Open access
  6. V. Vasas, C. Fernando, M. Santos, S. Kauffman & E. Szathmáry (2012). Evolution before genes. Biology Direct 7, 1. Open access
  7. W. Hordijk & M. Steel (2016). Autocatalytic sets in polymer networks with variable catalysis distributions. arXiv
  8. W. Hordijk & M. Steel (2017). Chasing the tail: the emergence of autocatalytic networks. BioSystems 152, 1–10. A review. Link

Heredity at the 7 confirmed points does not depend on the untemplated-growth cap, size-dependent washout, the random-number sampler or phase-resolved melting. It does depend on the copy step. Copying there is limited by free chain slots, and the engine fills them in a fixed order that serves the same templates again and again. With the slots filled in random order, heredity is lost at 2 of 7 points and halves at the rest. With copying shared out by rate, it survives at only 1–2 points. Paper 2 is revised with this limit. The vesicle and protocell levels were not re-tested. Results · Paper 2
