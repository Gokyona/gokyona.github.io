Appendix A — Glossary

This glossary lists the technical terms defined in the book, in alphabetical order. Each entry gives the term, its definition, and the chapter in which it is first used (or the Epilogue).

Terms defined in this book, with the chapter of first use.
Term Definition First used
5\('\) to 3\('\) direction The direction of a nucleic acid chain from the end with a free 5\('\) group to the end with a free 3\('\)-hydroxy group; sequences are written in this direction. 6
Abundance (cosmic) The relative amount of an element in a defined body or region, expressed as the number of atoms relative to hydrogen or as a mass fraction. 1
Acid A substance that donates a proton (H+) to another molecule. 2
Activated reactant A reactant that carries a built-in source of free energy, allowing a condensation to proceed in water. 4
Activation energy (Ea) The energy barrier that reactants must overcome to react; defined by IUPAC through the temperature dependence of the rate constant, Ea = RT2 d(ln k)/dT. 2
Activity The effective concentration of a dissolved species, corrected for interactions between particles, expressed relative to a standard concentration of 1 mol per litre. 2
Activity (of water) The effective concentration of water relative to pure water; it is lowered by evaporation and by concentration of solutes. 5
Addition reaction A reaction in which two molecules combine to form a single product without the loss of any atoms. 4
Adenine The purine C5H5N5; formally a pentamer of HCN. 4
Alanine The amino acid H2NCH(CH3)COOH (R = CH3); \(\beta\)-alanine, H2NCH2CH2COOH, is a structural isomer. 4
Alcohol A compound in which a hydroxy group, –OH, is attached to a carbon atom that forms only single bonds. 4
Aldehyde A compound containing the group –CHO, a carbon atom double-bonded to oxygen and bonded to hydrogen. 3
Aldol reaction Addition of the carbon atom next to the carbonyl group of one molecule, made nucleophilic in basic solution, to the carbonyl carbon of another, forming a carbon-carbon bond. 4
Aldose A sugar whose carbonyl group is an aldehyde at the end of the carbon chain. 4
Alkaline hydrothermal vent A hydrothermal vent, not driven directly by volcanic heat, that emits warm, alkaline fluids rich in H2 and CH4, such as the Lost City field. 3
Alpha particle A helium-4 nucleus, consisting of two protons and two neutrons. 1
Amide A compound derived from an acid by replacing a hydroxy group with an amino or substituted amino group; in carboxylic amides, –CO–NH2. 4
Amine A compound containing a nitrogen atom bonded to one or more carbon atoms and carrying a lone pair; the simplest group is the amino group, –NH2. 4
Amino acid A compound containing an amino group and a carboxy group; in the \(\alpha\)-amino acids of proteins both are attached to the same carbon atom, with general formula H2N–CHR–COOH. 4
Aminonitrile A compound containing an amino group and a nitrile group on the same carbon atom, RCH(NH2)CN; the intermediate of the Strecker synthesis. 4
2-Aminooxazole A small heterocycle formed from cyanamide and glycolaldehyde; an intermediate in the synthesis of pyrimidine nucleotides by Powner and co-workers. 5
Amphiphilic Having a polar part that interacts favourably with water and a non-polar part that does not. Epilogue
Ångström (Å) A unit of length equal to 10−10 m, convenient for bond lengths. 2
Anhydronucleoside A nucleoside in which an additional bond links the base to the sugar; an intermediate in the 2009 synthesis of pyrimidine nucleotides. 5
Annum (a) The year as a unit of time in geochronology; multiples are written ka (103 a), Ma (106 a) and Ga (109 a). 3
Anomer One of two stereoisomers of a ring-form sugar or nucleoside that differ only in the configuration at C1 (\(\alpha\) or \(\beta\)). 5
Antiparallel Of two paired nucleic acid strands: running in opposite 5\('\) to 3\('\) directions, the arrangement required for base pairing. 7
Apatite A group of calcium phosphate minerals of low solubility. 3
Aptamer A nucleic acid molecule, usually obtained by in vitro selection, that binds a specific target molecule. 7
Archean The second eon of Earth history, from 4031 ± 3 Ma to 2500 Ma. 3
Arrhenius equation k = A e−Ea/RT: the dependence of a rate constant k on absolute temperature T, with pre-exponential factor A and activation energy Ea. 2
Asymmetric autocatalysis Autocatalysis in which a chiral product catalyses the formation of itself rather than its enantiomer, so that an enantiomeric excess can be amplified. 4
Atom The smallest unit of a chemical element: a positively charged nucleus surrounded by electrons. 1
Atom ratio The number of atoms of one element in a sample divided by the number of atoms of another element in the same sample. 1
Atomic mass unit (u) One twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom; equivalent to 931.494 MeV of energy. 1
Atomic number (Z) The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom; it defines the element. 1
Autocatalysis Catalysis of a reaction by one of its own products. 2
Autotroph An organism that makes its organic matter from CO2. 3
Backbone The alternating sugar-phosphate chain of a nucleic acid, to which the nucleobases are attached. 6
Bar A unit of pressure equal to 105 Pa, close to the mean atmospheric pressure at sea level. 3
Base A substance that accepts a proton (H+) from another molecule. 2
Base pair Two nucleobases on the same or different strands joined by specific hydrogen bonds. 7
Base stacking The face-to-face packing of the flat rings of adjacent nucleobases along a helix, stabilised by dispersion forces and the hydrophobic effect. 7
Beta-plus decay Radioactive decay in which a proton in a nucleus becomes a neutron, emitting a positron and a neutrino. 1
Big Bang The model in which the Universe evolved from an extremely hot, dense initial state about 13.8 billion years ago and has been expanding and cooling since. 1
Big Bang nucleosynthesis The formation of the lightest nuclei (mainly hydrogen and helium-4, with traces of deuterium, helium-3 and lithium-7) in the first minutes after the Big Bang. 1
Binding energy The energy required to separate a nucleus completely into its protons and neutrons; equal to the mass difference between the free nucleons and the nucleus, multiplied by c2. 1
Bond angle The angle between two bonds that share an atom. 2
Bond enthalpy The enthalpy needed to break one mole of a given bond in molecules in the gas phase; e.g. 436.0 kJ mol−1 for H–H. 2
Bond length The distance between the nuclei of two atoms joined by a chemical bond. 2
Bond order The number of electron pairs shared between two bonded atoms: 1 for a single, 2 for a double and 3 for a triple bond. 2
Borate The anion of boric acid; it forms complexes with sugars that have hydroxy groups on adjacent carbon atoms. 4
Buffer (oxygen) An assemblage of minerals that fixes the oxygen fugacity at a known value at each temperature, such as iron-wüstite or fayalite-magnetite-quartz. 3
Bulge A loop between two helices with unpaired nucleotides on one strand only. 7
Calcium-aluminium-rich inclusion (CAI) A rare component of chondritic meteorites and the oldest dated solid of the solar system, 4.567-4.568 billion years old. 1
Carbonaceous chondrite A chondrite rich in carbon, often containing a wide variety of organic compounds; the Murchison meteorite is an example. 3
Carbonyl group A carbon atom double-bonded to an oxygen atom, C=O. 4
Carboxylic acid A compound containing the carboxy group, –COOH. 4
Catalyst A substance that increases the rate of a reaction without changing its overall standard Gibbs energy change; it is regenerated in the reaction. 2
Chandrasekhar mass The largest mass that the pressure of electrons can support against gravity in a stellar core; when an iron core exceeds it, the core collapses. 1
Chirality The property of an object of being non-superposable on its mirror image. 4
Chondrite A meteorite consisting of unprocessed early solar-system material: CAIs, chondrules and iron-nickel metal in a fine-grained matrix. 1
Chondrule An abundant, rounded, magnesium- and silicon-rich component of chondritic meteorites, formed up to about 4 million years after CAIs. 1
Clay mineral A layered silicate formed by the weathering of rocks in water. 6
Closed system (geochronology) A sample to which no parent or daughter atoms have been added and from which none have been removed since the event being dated. 3
CNO cycle A cycle of nuclear reactions in which carbon, nitrogen and oxygen nuclei catalyse the conversion of four protons into one helium-4 nucleus; the main stellar source of nitrogen-14. 1
Compartment A closed boundary that holds a set of molecules together and separates them from their surroundings. Epilogue
Complementary Of two sequences: able to form Watson-Crick base pairs along their whole length when aligned antiparallel. 7
Concordant age A uranium-lead age for which the 238U-206Pb and 235U-207Pb ages agree within uncertainty. 3
Condensation reaction A reaction in which two reactants (or two sites in one molecule) yield one main product together with water or another small molecule. 4
Continental crust The outer layer of rock that forms the continents; its upper part is the most accessible region of the solid Earth. 1
Core electrons The electrons in the inner, completely filled shells of an atom; they are tightly bound and do not take part in bonding. 2
Core-collapse supernova The explosion of a star of more than about 8 solar masses, triggered by the collapse of its iron core. 1
Cosmic microwave background Nearly uniform microwave radiation filling the sky, released when the Universe first became transparent at recombination and now cooled to about 2.7 K. 1
Covalent bond A chemical bond formed by a pair of electrons shared between two atoms. 2
Cyanamide H2NCN, an amino group bonded to a nitrile group; a prebiotic feedstock molecule. 3
Cyanoacetylene HC\(\equiv\)C–C\(\equiv\)N, a product of spark discharge in CH4/N2 mixtures and a precursor of pyrimidines. 4
Cyanohydrin A compound in which a hydroxy group and a nitrile group are attached to the same carbon atom, formed by addition of HCN to a carbonyl group. 4
2\('\),3\('\)-Cyclic phosphate A nucleotide in which one phosphate bridges C2\('\) and C3\('\) in a five-membered ring; an activated form that can form phosphodiester bonds. 5
Cytosine The pyrimidine C4H5N3O; it is converted to uracil by hydrolysis. 4
Daughter (nuclide) The nuclide formed by the radioactive decay of a parent nuclide. 3
Debye (D) The customary unit of molecular dipole moment; 1 D = 3.335 64 × 10−30 C m. 2
Decay (radioactive) The spontaneous transformation of an unstable nucleus into a different nucleus, with emission of radiation. 1
Decay constant (\(\lambda\)) The probability per unit time that a nucleus of a given radioactive nuclide decays; related to the half-life by t1/2 = ln 2/\(\lambda\). 3
Delta notation (\(\delta\)) The deviation of an isotope ratio in a sample from that of a standard, (\(R\)sample/\(R\)standard − 1) × 1000, expressed in per mil (‰). 3
Detrital Describes a mineral grain eroded from its original rock and incorporated into a sediment. 3
Deuterium bottleneck The delay in Big Bang nucleosynthesis caused by the destruction of newly formed deuterons by energetic photons, lasting until the Universe cooled to about 109 K. 1
Deuteron The nucleus of deuterium, consisting of one proton and one neutron. 1
Diamidophosphate (DAP) PO2(NH2)2−, a phosphorylating agent that acts in water without an additional condensing agent. 4
Diaminomaleonitrile (DAMN) The tetramer of HCN, an intermediate in the formation of adenine from HCN. 4
Differentiation (planetary) The melting of a planetary body and its internal separation into layers, such as a metallic core and a rocky mantle. 1
Dipole moment (\(\mu\)) For charges +q and −q separated by a distance d, the product \(\mu = qd\); a measure of the separation of charge in a molecule. 2
Dipole-dipole interaction The attraction between the positive end of one polar molecule and the negative end of another. 2
Dispersion force A weak attraction between temporary, mutually induced dipoles in neighbouring atoms or molecules; it acts between all molecules and grows with molecular size. 2
Dot-bracket notation A notation for nested secondary structure: a string with a bracket for each paired nucleotide (opening for the 5\('\) partner, closing for the 3\('\) partner) and a dot for each unpaired nucleotide. 7
Double bond A covalent bond in which two atoms share two pairs of electrons. 2
Double helix The right-handed helical structure formed by two antiparallel, base-paired nucleic acid strands or segments. 7
Doubling time (td) The time in which an exponentially growing population doubles in size. 8
Dry mass The mass of biological material remaining after all water has been removed. 1
Duplex A complex of two separate nucleic acid strands held together by base pairs. 7
Dynamic programming A method that solves a problem by combining stored solutions of smaller sub-problems; used to find minimum free energy RNA structures. 7
Electrical discharge The passage of electric current through a gas, as in lightning, which heats the gas and breaks its molecules into fragments. 3
Electron A subatomic particle with one negative elementary charge; electrons occupy orbitals around the nucleus and determine chemical behaviour. 1
Electron configuration A list of the number of electrons in each subshell of an atom, such as 1s2 2s2 2p2 for carbon. 2
Electronegativity (\(\chi\)) A measure of the tendency of an atom in a bond to attract the shared electrons towards itself; on the Pauling scale, H 2.20, C 2.55, N 3.04, O 3.44, P 2.19. 2
Electronvolt (eV) The energy gained by a particle with one elementary charge moving through a potential difference of one volt; exactly 1.602 176 634 × 10−19 J. 2
Electrophile A reagent that forms a bond by accepting both bonding electrons from its reaction partner. 4
Element A substance consisting of atoms that all have the same atomic number. 1
Enantiomeric cross-inhibition Inhibition of template-directed chain growth by monomers of the opposite handedness, which are incorporated and block further extension. 5
Enantiomeric excess (ee) ([L] − [D])/([L] + [D]), the fractional excess of one enantiomer in a mixture; 0 for a racemate, ±1 for a homochiral sample. 4
Enantiomers Two molecules that are non-superposable mirror images of each other. 4
Endothermic Describes a process that absorbs heat from its surroundings (ΔH > 0). 2
Energy level One of the discrete values of energy that an electron in an atom or molecule can have. 2
Enthalpy (H) Internal energy of a system plus the product of its pressure and volume; its change equals the heat exchanged at constant pressure. 2
Entropy (S) A thermodynamic quantity measuring the number of microscopic arrangements consistent with the macroscopic state of a system. 2
Enzyme A biological catalyst; a protein or, in the case of a ribozyme, an RNA molecule. 7
Eon The largest unit of geological time; Earth history is divided into the Hadean, Archean, Proterozoic and Phanerozoic eons. 3
Equilibrium (chemical) The state of a reversible reaction in which the forward and reverse reactions proceed at equal rates, so that the amounts of reactants and products no longer change. 2
Equilibrium constant (K) The ratio, at equilibrium, of the activities of products to those of reactants, each raised to the power of its coefficient; related to \(\Delta G^\circ\) by \(\Delta G^\circ = -RT \ln K\). 4
Error catastrophe The loss of the information of a master sequence among its mutants when the error rate exceeds the error threshold. 8
Error rate per nucleotide (u) The probability that a nucleotide is copied incorrectly; the fidelity is q = 1 − u. 8
Error threshold The greatest sequence length, Lmax \(\approx\) ln \(\sigma\)/u, that can be maintained by replication with error rate u and selective superiority \(\sigma\). 8
Ester A compound formally derived from an acid and an alcohol by linking them with the loss of water. 4
Eutectic phase The concentrated liquid that remains between ice crystals when a solution is cooled below its freezing point. 6
Excited state A state of a nucleus, atom or molecule with more energy than its lowest-energy state. 1
Exogenous delivery The arrival on the Earth of organic molecules already present in extraterrestrial objects. 3
Exothermic Describes a process that releases heat to its surroundings (ΔH < 0). 2
Exterior loop The unpaired nucleotides of a secondary structure that are not enclosed by any base pair, including the two chain ends. 7
Faint young Sun problem The difficulty of reconciling the lower luminosity of the young Sun, about 30 percent below the present value, with evidence for liquid water on the early Earth. 3
Fatty acid A carboxylic acid with a long hydrocarbon chain; an amphiphilic molecule used in models of protocell membranes. Epilogue
Feedstock molecule A small, simple molecule from which more complex molecules can be built in a sequence of reactions. 3
Fidelity (q) The probability that a nucleotide is copied correctly. 8
Formaldehyde H2CO, the simplest aldehyde; the feedstock of the formose reaction. 3
Formamide HCONH2, the amide of formic acid; a liquid from 2.6 °C to about 211 °C, from which nucleobases form on heating with mineral catalysts. 4
Formamidopyrimidine A pyrimidine carrying a formamido group, whose atoms can close a second ring to give a purine; an intermediate in prebiotic purine nucleoside syntheses. 5
Formose reaction The conversion of formaldehyde, in the presence of a base, into a mixture of sugars; it is autocatalytic. 3
Freeze-out The point at which a reaction becomes too slow, relative to the expansion of the Universe, to keep the amounts of its products in equilibrium. 1
Functional group An atom or group of atoms that has similar chemical properties whenever it occurs in different compounds. 4
Furanose The five-membered ring form of a sugar (four carbon atoms and one oxygen atom), formed by addition of a hydroxy group to the carbonyl group of the same molecule. 4
G\(\cdot\)U wobble pair A base pair between guanine and uracil held by two hydrogen bonds, with the bases shifted relative to a Watson-Crick pair; common in RNA helices. 7
Gamma ray (\(\gamma\)) A photon of very high energy, typically emitted in nuclear reactions. 1
Geometric distribution The distribution P(n) = (1 − p)pn−1 of chain lengths when each extension succeeds with probability p; its mean is 1/(1 − p). 6
Giant impact hypothesis The hypothesis that the Moon formed from debris thrown into orbit when a body about the size of Mars collided with the proto-Earth. 3
Gibbs free energy (G) G = H - TS. At constant temperature and pressure, a process occurs spontaneously only if it lowers G. 2
Glycine The simplest amino acid, H2NCH2COOH (R = H); the only common amino acid without a stereocentre. 4
Graphite A crystalline form of pure carbon. 3
Ground state The lowest-energy state of a nucleus, atom or molecule. 1
Guanine The purine C5H5N5O. 4
Hadean The first eon of Earth history, from the formation of the solar system to 4031 ± 3 Ma. 3
Hairpin loop A loop of unpaired nucleotides closed by a single base pair at the end of a helix; it contains at least three nucleotides. 7
Half-life The time required for half of the nuclei in a sample of a radioactive isotope to decay. 1
Helioseismology The study of the interior of the Sun through its oscillations, observed at its surface. 1
Helium burning Nuclear fusion of helium in a stellar core, producing mainly carbon-12 and oxygen-16; it requires temperatures near 108 K. 1
Helium mass fraction (Y) The fraction of the total mass of ordinary matter that is in the form of helium-4. 1
Helix (stem) In secondary structure, a run of consecutive stacked base pairs. 7
Hess’s law The enthalpy change of a reaction is independent of the route taken, so it can be calculated from tabulated enthalpies of formation. 2
Heterocyclic compound A cyclic compound whose ring contains atoms of at least one element other than carbon. 4
Homochirality The property of consisting of only one of two enantiomers, as RNA consists only of D-ribose units. 4
Hoyle state The excited state of carbon-12 at 7.65 MeV, through which the triple-alpha process proceeds. 1
Hund’s rule In a partly filled subshell, electrons occupy separate orbitals with parallel spins before any orbital receives a second electron. 2
Hybridisation The description of a bonded atom’s valence orbitals as mixtures of its s and p orbitals (sp3, sp2, sp) that point in the directions of its bonds. 2
Hydration shell The layer of oriented water molecules surrounding a dissolved ion or molecule. 2
Hydrogen bond An attractive interaction between a hydrogen atom of a group X–H, in which X is more electronegative than H, and an atom or group of atoms in the same or another molecule, in which there is evidence of bond formation (IUPAC 2011); written X–H···Y. 2
Hydrogen burning Nuclear fusion of hydrogen into helium in a star; a nuclear, not a chemical, process. 1
Hydrogen cyanide HCN, a linear molecule with a carbon-nitrogen triple bond; a feedstock for nucleobases. 3
Hydrogen-bond acceptor The atom Y, usually O or N with a lone pair, that receives a hydrogen bond X–H···Y. 2
Hydrogen-bond donor The atom X to which the hydrogen of a hydrogen bond X–H···Y is covalently bonded. 2
Hydrolysis The breaking of a bond by reaction with water; the reverse of a condensation. 4
Hydronium ion The ion H3O+, formed when a water molecule accepts a proton. 2
Hydrophilic Interacting favourably with water; describes polar and charged molecules or groups. 2
Hydrophobic effect The tendency of non-polar molecules or groups to cluster together in water, reducing their contact with water. 2
Hydrothermal vent A site on the sea floor where water heated within the crust flows out into the ocean. 3
Hydroxide ion The ion OH−, formed when a water molecule loses a proton. 2
Imidazole A five-membered aromatic ring containing two nitrogen atoms, C3H4N2. 6
Imine A compound containing a carbon-nitrogen double bond, R2C=NR; formed by condensation of an aldehyde or ketone with ammonia or an amine. 4
Impact-shock synthesis The formation of organic molecules by shock heating during the entry or impact of extraterrestrial objects. 3
In vitro selection Repeated cycles of selecting molecules with a desired property from a pool of random sequences and amplifying them. 7
Intermolecular force An attraction between molecules, much weaker than a covalent bond, such as dipole-dipole, dispersion and hydrogen-bond interactions. 2
Internal loop A loop between two helices with unpaired nucleotides on both strands. 7
Interplanetary dust particle (IDP) A small particle of extraterrestrial dust that falls on the Earth from space. 3
Ion An atom or molecule with a net electric charge, resulting from an unequal number of protons and electrons. 1
Ion product of water (Kw) The product of the activities of H3O+ and OH− in water at equilibrium; pKw = 13.99 at 25 °C. 2
Ionic bond A bond formed by the electrostatic attraction between ions of opposite charge, resulting from the transfer of electrons between atoms of very different electronegativity. 2
Ionisation energy (first) The minimum energy required to remove one electron from a neutral atom in its ground state. 2
Isomers Species with the same molecular formula but different structures. 4
Isotope Atoms of the same element (same atomic number) that differ in their number of neutrons. 1
Isotope fractionation The partial separation of isotopes of different mass by physical or chemical processes. 3
Ketone A compound in which a carbonyl group is bonded to two carbon atoms. 4
Ketose A sugar whose carbonyl group is a ketone. 4
Late Heavy Bombardment Impacts on the planets and the Moon after their outer layers became rigid enough to preserve craters and basins; in particular, an episode of elevated impact rate between about 4.2 to 4.0 Ga and 3.5 Ga. 3
Late veneer Material accreted by the Earth after the Moon-forming impact. 3
Lead-lead age An age calculated from the ratio of radiogenic 207Pb to radiogenic 206Pb, which depends on the time elapsed but not on the amount of uranium in the sample. 3
Leaving group The group that departs with an electron pair when a nucleophile attacks; a good leaving group makes the reaction easier. 6
Lewis structure A diagram of a molecule in which shared electron pairs are drawn as lines and lone pairs as pairs of dots. 2
Log epsilon (log \(\varepsilon\)) An abundance scale defined as log10(NX/NH) + 12, where NX and NH are the numbers of atoms of element X and of hydrogen; hydrogen has 12 by definition. 1
Lone pair A pair of valence electrons that belongs to one atom and is not shared in a bond. 2
Luminosity The total power emitted by a star, integrated over all wavelengths; the present luminosity of the Sun is 3.85 × 1026 W. 3
Magma ocean A global layer of molten rock at the surface of a planet or moon. 3
Mass fraction The mass of one component of a sample divided by the total mass of the sample. 1
Mass number (A) The total number of protons and neutrons in a nucleus. 1
Master sequence In quasispecies theory, the sequence that replicates fastest; its mutants replicate more slowly. 8
Melting temperature (Tm) The temperature at which half of the molecules are folded or paired; in the two-state model Tm = \(\Delta\)H/\(\Delta\)S. 7
Meteorite An extraterrestrial body that survives passage through the atmosphere and reaches the ground. 3
Micelle A small aggregate of amphiphilic molecules in water, with the non-polar parts inside and the polar parts facing the water. Epilogue
Minimum free energy (MFE) structure The secondary structure of lowest Gibbs energy that a sequence can form under a given energy model. 7
Molal (mol kg−1) A concentration expressed as moles of solute per kilogram of solvent. 3
Molar (M) A concentration expressed as moles of solute per litre of solution. 3
Mole The amount of substance containing exactly 6.022 140 76 × 1023 elementary entities (atoms, molecules, ions). 2
Molecular cloud A cold, dense cloud of interstellar gas, in which hydrogen is present mainly as H2 molecules, together with dust; stars form in such clouds. 1
Molecule Two or more atoms held together by covalent bonds and acting as a single unit. 2
Monoisotopic element An element that has only one stable isotope, such as phosphorus (31P). 1
Monosaccharide (sugar) A compound Cn(H2O)n, usually with n from 3 to 7, containing one carbonyl group and a hydroxy group on each other carbon atom. 4
Montmorillonite A layered clay mineral whose negatively charged aluminium silicate layers adsorb organic molecules; it catalyses the oligomerisation of activated nucleotides. 6
Multibranch loop A loop from which three or more helices radiate; also called a junction. 7
N-glycosidic bond The bond between C1\('\) of the sugar and a ring nitrogen of the base (N9 in purines, N1 in pyrimidines) in a nucleoside. 5
Natural selection The increase in frequency of variants that replicate faster, more accurately or for longer, among replicators competing for limited resources. 8
Nearest-neighbour model An energy model in which the Gibbs energy of a helix is the sum of terms for each pair of adjacent base pairs plus an initiation term. 7
Neutral atmosphere An atmosphere in which carbon and nitrogen are present mainly as CO2 and N2, without much H2 or free O2. 3
Neutrino An electrically neutral elementary particle of very small mass that interacts only weakly with matter. 1
Neutron An electrically neutral subatomic particle found in the nucleus. 1
Nitrile A compound containing the group –C\(\equiv\)N. 4
Nuclear fusion A nuclear reaction in which two light nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus. For nuclei lighter than iron, fusion releases energy. 1
Nucleobase A nitrogen-containing heterocyclic compound that, bonded to ribose, forms part of a nucleotide; in RNA, adenine, guanine, cytosine and uracil. 4
Nucleon A proton or a neutron. 1
Nucleophile A reagent that forms a bond by donating an electron pair to its reaction partner. 4
Nucleoside A nucleobase joined to a sugar (ribose in RNA), without a phosphate group. 3
Nucleosynthesis The formation of atomic nuclei by nuclear reactions. 1
Nucleotide A nucleoside carrying one or more phosphate groups, usually on C5\('\) of the sugar; the monomer unit of RNA. 5
Nucleus (atomic) The small, dense, positively charged centre of an atom, made of protons and neutrons. 1
Octet rule The tendency of carbon, nitrogen and oxygen to form bonds until each has eight valence electrons, the configuration of neon. 2
Oligonucleotide A short chain of nucleotides, often described by its length, as in a 10-mer; a long chain is a polynucleotide. 6
Orbital A region around a nucleus, described by a wavefunction, in which an electron of a given energy is likely to be found; each orbital holds at most two electrons. 2
Oxidation The loss of electrons by an atom, ion or molecule; an increase in oxidation number. 3
Oxidation number The charge an atom would carry if the shared electrons of each of its covalent bonds were assigned to the more electronegative atom. 3
Oxygen fugacity The effective pressure of O2 in equilibrium with a magma or other system; a measure of how oxidising it is. 3
Parent (nuclide) A radioactive nuclide that decays into a daughter nuclide. 3
Partition function (Z) The sum of exp(\(-\Delta\)G/RT) over all structures of a molecule; the probability of a structure is its term divided by Z. 7
Pauli exclusion principle No two electrons in an atom can be in the same quantum state; hence an orbital holds at most two electrons, with opposite spins. 2
Pentose A sugar with five carbon atoms; ribose is an aldopentose. 4
Per mil (‰) Parts per thousand. 3
pH The negative base-10 logarithm of the activity of hydronium ions in a solution; neutral pH equals pKw/2, which is 7.00 at 25 °C. 2
Phosphate The ion PO43−, in which phosphorus is bonded tetrahedrally to four oxygen atoms; also used for groups derived from it. 3
Phosphate ester An ester formed from phosphoric acid and an alcohol; the units of RNA are joined by phosphate ester linkages. 4
Phosphate problem The mismatch between the low concentrations of phosphate in natural waters and the high concentrations used in prebiotic experiments. 3
Phosphoanhydride bond A P–O–P linkage formed by condensation of two phosphate groups, as in pyrophosphate and ATP; its hydrolysis releases more free energy than that of a phosphate ester. 4
Phosphodiester bond The phosphate diester linkage that joins the C3 hydroxy group of one ribose to the C5 hydroxy group of the next in RNA. 4
Phosphoric acid H3PO4, a triprotic acid; its anions are collectively called phosphate. 4
Phosphorimidazolide A nucleotide in which one oxygen of the phosphate is replaced by a nitrogen atom of imidazole or a substituted imidazole; an activated monomer. 6
Phosphorylation The attachment of a phosphate group to a molecule. 4
Photochemistry Chemical reactions initiated by the absorption of light. 3
Photosphere The layer of a star from which its visible light escapes into space; stellar abundances are measured from absorption lines formed there. 1
Phytoplankton Microscopic photosynthetic organisms living in the surface waters of oceans and lakes. 1
Pi (\(\pi\)) bond A covalent bond formed by side-by-side overlap of two parallel p orbitals, with electron density above and below the line joining the nuclei. 2
Plasma A gas in which a significant fraction of atoms are ionised, so that it contains free electrons and positively charged nuclei or ions. 1
Polar bond A covalent bond in which the shared electrons are unequally distributed because the two atoms differ in electronegativity; the ends carry partial charges \(\delta+\) and \(\delta-\). 2
Polar molecule A molecule with a net dipole moment, because the dipoles of its polar bonds do not cancel. 2
Polyphosphate A chain or ring of phosphate units joined by phosphoanhydride bonds, such as pyrophosphate or trimetaphosphate. 4
Positron The antiparticle of the electron, with the same mass and opposite (positive) charge. 1
Primer A short strand bound to a template and extended at its 3\('\) end during copying. 8
Principal quantum number (n) The number (1, 2, 3, …) that labels an electron shell; higher n means electrons that are on average farther from the nucleus. 2
Protocell A compartment, such as a vesicle, that contains a replicating genetic system; a model of an early cell. Epilogue
Proton A subatomic particle with one positive elementary charge, found in the nucleus. 1
Proton-proton chain The sequence of fusion reactions that converts four protons into one helium-4 nucleus, two positrons and two neutrinos; the main energy source of the Sun. 1
Protoplanetary disc A flattened, rotating disc of gas and dust around a young star, from which planets form. 1
Pseudoknot A structure containing base pairs that cross rather than nest: pairs (i, j) and (k, l) with i < k < j < l. 7
Purine A nucleobase with a six-membered ring fused to a five-membered ring, nine ring atoms of which four are nitrogen; adenine and guanine. 4
Pyranose The six-membered ring form of a sugar (five carbon atoms and one oxygen atom). 4
Pyrimidine A nucleobase with a single six-membered ring containing two nitrogen atoms; cytosine and uracil (thymine in DNA). 4
Quantum mechanics The physical theory that describes the behaviour of matter and energy on the scale of atoms and subatomic particles. 2
Quasispecies The population formed by a master sequence and the mutants generated by its error-prone replication. 8
Racemate A mixture containing equal amounts of two enantiomers. 4
Radiogenic Produced by radioactive decay; used of daughter atoms formed within a sample. 3
Radiometric age The age of a sample calculated from the measured ratio of radiogenic daughter to parent atoms and the decay constant of the parent. 3
Rate constant (k) The proportionality factor between the rate of a reaction and the concentrations of its reactants; it depends on temperature but not on concentration. 2
Rate of reaction The rate of change of the concentration of a reactant or product with time, divided by its coefficient in the reaction equation. 2
Recombination The epoch, about 380,000 years after the Big Bang, when electrons first bound permanently to nuclei to form neutral atoms. 1
Redfield ratio The average atom ratio C:N:P = 106:16:1 used as a reference for the composition of marine organisms. 1
Redox couple A reducing and an oxidising substance that can react with each other by electron transfer, such as H2 and CO2. 3
Redox reaction A reaction in which one species is oxidised and another reduced. 3
Reducing atmosphere An atmosphere in which carbon and nitrogen are present mainly in low oxidation states, as CH4 and NH3, together with H2. 3
Reduction The gain of electrons by an atom, ion or molecule; a decrease in oxidation number. 3
Relative atomic mass (Ar) The average mass of the atoms of an element in a given sample, expressed in atomic mass units; the standard atomic weight is its recommended value for normal materials. 1
Relative permittivity (\(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}}\)) The factor by which a medium reduces the electrostatic force between charges compared with a vacuum; 78.4 for water at 25 °C. 2
Replication The production of a copy of a sequence; for nucleic acids, two rounds of template-directed synthesis separated by strand separation. 8
Resonance (nuclear) A strong enhancement of a nuclear reaction rate when the energy of the colliding nuclei matches the energy of an excited state of the product nucleus. 1
Ribose The aldopentose C5H10O5 of RNA; RNA contains only D-ribose, in the furanose form. 4
Ribosome The complex of RNA and protein on which cells make proteins; its peptide-bond-forming site consists of RNA. Epilogue
Ribozyme An RNA molecule that acts as a catalyst. 7
Ribozyme polymerase A ribozyme that extends an RNA primer on an RNA template; so far obtained only by laboratory evolution. 8
RNA world hypothesis The hypothesis that, at an early stage of life, RNA served both as genetic material and as the main catalyst. 8
Schreibersite The meteoritic mineral (Fe,Ni)3P, in which phosphorus is bonded to metal atoms; it releases reactive phosphorus compounds when it corrodes in water. 3
Second law of thermodynamics In any spontaneous process, the total entropy of a system and its surroundings increases. 2
Secondary structure The set of base pairs formed by a nucleic acid molecule. 7
Selective superiority (\(\sigma\)) The ratio of the replication rate of the master sequence to that of its mutants. 8
Selectivity The extent to which a reaction or process forms one product, or acts on one compound, in preference to others. 4
SELEX Systematic evolution of ligands by exponential enrichment: the in vitro selection procedure of Tuerk and Gold (1990). 7
Sequence space The set of all sequences of a given length; for RNA of length n it contains 4n sequences. 7
Shell (electron) The set of orbitals in an atom with the same principal quantum number n; it holds at most 2n2 electrons. 2
Short-lived radionuclide A radioactive isotope with a half-life of less than 200 million years that was present in the early solar system and is now extinct. 1
Sigma (\(\sigma\)) bond A covalent bond formed by head-on overlap of orbitals, with electron density concentrated along the line joining the nuclei. 2
Snow line The distance from a young star beyond which ice of a given substance can form; for water the corresponding temperature is about 150 K. 1
Spark discharge experiment A laboratory simulation in which an electric discharge acts on a gas mixture in contact with water, as in the experiment of Miller (1953). 4
Spin An intrinsic property of the electron that can take one of two values, conventionally called up and down. 2
Splicing The removal of an intervening sequence from an RNA precursor and the joining of the flanking pieces. 7
Stack In the nearest-neighbour model, a pair of adjacent base pairs in a helix. 7
Stalling The slowing of primer extension after a mismatched nucleotide has been incorporated. 8
Standard enthalpy of formation (ΔfH°) The enthalpy change when one mole of a compound is formed from its elements in their most stable forms, all in their standard states. 2
Star A self-gravitating ball of gas in which nuclear fusion in the core supplies the energy radiated from the surface. 1
Stereocentre An atom, usually carbon bonded to four different groups, at which exchanging two groups gives a stereoisomer. 4
Strand-separation problem The difficulty that the product of template copying is bound to its template more strongly than primers and monomers, so that releasing it requires conditions that damage RNA. 8
Strecker synthesis Formation of an amino acid from an aldehyde, ammonia and hydrogen cyanide, through an imine and an aminonitrile that is then hydrolysed. 4
Strong nuclear force The fundamental force that binds protons and neutrons together in a nucleus; it is much stronger than the electrical repulsion between protons but acts only over distances of about 10−15 m. 1
Structural isomers Isomers that differ in the order in which their atoms are bonded. 4
Subshell A group of orbitals within a shell with the same shape, labelled s, p, d or f and containing 1, 3, 5 or 7 orbitals. 2
Supernova The explosive death of a star, which ejects newly synthesised elements into interstellar space. 1
Supernova remnant The expanding cloud of gas ejected by a supernova explosion, together with the interstellar gas it sweeps up. 1
Template An existing nucleotide chain whose sequence directs the order in which monomers are joined. 5
Tertiary structure The three-dimensional arrangement of the atoms of a folded molecule. 7
Tetrahedral angle The angle between lines from the centre of a regular tetrahedron to two of its corners, arccos(−1/3), approximately 109.47°. 2
Transition state The arrangement of atoms at the point of highest energy along the path from reactants to products. 2
Triple bond A covalent bond in which two atoms share three pairs of electrons. 2
Triple-alpha process The fusion of three helium-4 nuclei into carbon-12 inside stars, proceeding via unstable beryllium-8. 1
Ultraviolet light Electromagnetic radiation with wavelengths shorter than those of visible light, below about 400 nm. 3
Uracil The pyrimidine C4H4N2O2; it pairs with adenine in RNA. 4
Valence The number of covalent bonds an atom usually forms: 1 for H, 4 for C, 3 for N and 2 for O. 2
Valence electrons The electrons in the outermost shell of an atom, which take part in chemical bonding. 2
Van der Waals forces The collective name for dipole-dipole, dipole-induced dipole and dispersion interactions between molecules. 2
Vesicle A closed membrane, made of a double layer of amphiphilic molecules, that encloses a volume of water. Epilogue
VSEPR Valence-shell electron-pair repulsion: a model in which the electron pairs around an atom arrange themselves as far apart as possible, determining molecular shape. 2
Watson-Crick base pair The base pairs A–U (A–T in DNA), with two hydrogen bonds, and G–C, with three. 7
Wet-dry cycle Repeated drying and rewetting of a small body of water, which concentrates dissolved molecules and promotes the joining of building blocks into chains. 3
Zircon The mineral zirconium silicate, ZrSiO4; it contains trace uranium, resists weathering, and is the main source of information about the Hadean Earth. 3
