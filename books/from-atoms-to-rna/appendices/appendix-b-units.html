Appendix B — Units, Constants and Orders of Magnitude

This appendix collects the units, physical constants and conversion factors used in the book, and lists characteristic magnitudes that appear in the chapters.

B.1 SI units and prefixes

The book uses the International System of Units (SI). Table B.1 lists the base and derived units that occur most often, and Table B.2 the prefixes used to form multiples.

Several units outside the SI are used in the book because they are standard in their fields:

  • Degree Celsius (°C). A temperature t in °C corresponds to the absolute temperature T = t + 273.15 K. A temperature difference of 1 °C equals a difference of 1 K.
  • Litre (L), equal to 10−3 m3.
  • Bar, equal to 105 Pa; the standard atmosphere (atm) is 101 325 Pa exactly (National Institute of Standards and Technology, n.d.).
  • Electronvolt (eV), the energy gained by an elementary charge across a potential difference of 1 V, equal to 1.602 176 634 × 10−19 J exactly (National Institute of Standards and Technology, n.d.).
  • Annum (a), the year used in geology and astronomy, with the multiples ka, Ma and Ga (Section 3.1).
  • Kilocalorie (kcal). The nearest-neighbour parameters of Chapter 7 are published in kcal mol−1; the conversion used in this book is 1 kcal = 4.184 kJ (the thermochemical calorie).
  • Unified atomic mass unit (u), also called the dalton (Da), equal to one twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12; its value is the atomic mass constant in Table B.3.

B.2 Physical constants

Table B.3 lists the constants used in the book, with their values from the 2022 CODATA adjustment (National Institute of Standards and Technology, n.d.). Values marked exact follow from the definition of the SI; for the Boltzmann constant in eV K−1, the molar gas constant and the Faraday constant, which are products or quotients of exact constants, the digits shown are truncated. The last five values are measured, and the digits in parentheses give the standard uncertainty in the last digits.

B.3 Useful conversions

Chemical energies are given in the book per mole, physical energies per particle. The two are related by the Avogadro constant:

\[ E_\text{molar} = N_\mathrm{A} \, E_\text{particle} \tag{B.1}\]

From the values of Table B.3 follow the conversions and reference values of Table B.4.

The quantity RT sets the scale of thermal energy per mole. By Equation 7.2, a free-energy difference of a few RT changes the population of a state by an order of magnitude or more: a difference of RT ln 10 changes it tenfold.
